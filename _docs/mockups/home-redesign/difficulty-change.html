Difficulty. Change Interactions

3 options · what happens when the user wants to change level

Three ways to handle "I want to change my difficulty." Each shows the moment the user has just tapped to change — what they see next. Resting state is the same 5-tick bar in all three.

Option 1

Inline expand

"Change ↗" expands a panel right under the bar. List of 5 levels with name, descriptor, and lock state. Tap one — bar updates above, panel collapses. Stays on home.

Trade-off: feels lightweight, stays in flow. Limit: 5 levels is the max this pattern handles before it gets long.

Option 2

Bottom sheet

"Change ↗" slides up an iOS-native bottom sheet. Full list with more breathing room, locked tiers can show "Unlock with Pro" inline, room for a primary Confirm button.

Trade-off: most discoverable, most iOS-familiar. Cost: one extra interaction layer; user briefly leaves home context.

Option 3

Tap the bar

No "Change" link, no expand, no sheet. Each tick is a tappable chip with its range labeled. Tap a tier — it becomes the current. Descriptor below updates inline.

Trade-off: zero friction, one tap to change. Cost: ticks become bigger (still calm but not invisible). Less room for descriptor copy per tier.
